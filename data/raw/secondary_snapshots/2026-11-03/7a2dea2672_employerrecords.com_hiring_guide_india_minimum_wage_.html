Key takeaways

  1. No single national rate. Each state sets minimums by skill level and zone. For unskilled work in the main hiring states they run from ₹10,558 a month (West Bengal Zone A) to ₹24,407 (Bengaluru).
  2. The labour codes are in force. Since 21 November 2025 the minimum wage covers every employee, a 50% rule sets the base for PF and gratuity, and fixed-term staff earn gratuity after one year.
  3. The wage is only the floor. At the Delhi unskilled minimum of ₹18,456, PF, ESI, gratuity and bonus add about ₹5,423 a month, taking the employer cost to ₹23,879.
  4. Rates move twice a year. Central rates rose on 1 April and 1 October 2026 (Area A unskilled: ₹827 to ₹840 a day), and the EPF wage ceiling rose to ₹25,000 on 17 September 2026.

01 · How it works

How minimum wage works in India in 2026

India has no single national minimum wage. Each state and union territory sets its own rates, and the central government sets separate rates for employment in its own sphere, such as central public sector units, railways, mines, ports and airports. To see how these rates compare internationally, our global minimum wage statistics put 195 countries on one monthly basis.

Within a state, the rate depends on four things: the employee’s place of work, their skill category (unskilled, semi-skilled, skilled or highly skilled), the zone (usually city, town or rural) and, where the state still uses them, the scheduled employment or industry. A private company hiring office staff in Bengaluru follows Karnataka’s rates for its Zone 1, not the central rates.

Four checks before you set a salary

Use the state where the employee actually works, not the company’s registered office. Classify the role against the state’s skill definitions rather than the job title. Check the zone, since Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana, Gujarat, West Bengal and Uttar Pradesh all set different rates by area. Then confirm you are using the latest notification, because most states add a variable dearness allowance (VDA) every six months.

What counts toward the minimum. Compliance is tested against “wages” as the Code on Wages defines them: basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance. House rent allowance, conveyance and similar allowances do not count, so a basic salary padded out with allowances can fall below the minimum even when the gross looks generous.

02 · The labour codes

The Code on Wages and the labour codes in 2026

All four labour codes came into force on 21 November 2025, according to the Ministry of Labour and Employment: the Code on Wages 2019, the Industrial Relations Code 2020, the Code on Social Security 2020 and the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code 2020. Together they replaced 29 central labour laws, including the Minimum Wages Act 1948, the Payment of Wages Act 1936 and the Payment of Bonus Act 1965.

The final Code on Wages (Central) Rules were notified on 8 May 2026, as were the central rules under the safety code. States are at different stages: by May 2026 Maharashtra, Gujarat, Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh and Delhi had notified rules under all four codes, while West Bengal had not notified any.

03 · Floor wage

The national floor wage

India’s floor wage is still the non-statutory National Floor Level Minimum Wage of ₹176 a day, last revised in 2017. A rise to ₹178 was announced in July 2019 alongside the Code on Wages Bill but was never formally notified.

The Code on Wages makes the floor wage statutory: once notified, no state may set a minimum wage below it. In July 2026 the Labour Ministry began work on the figure, updating the consumption basket and setting up a 15-member Central Advisory Board, and the 2026 central rules allow the floor to be revised every five years. No date has been announced.

04 · Central sphere

Central sphere minimum wages after the April and October 2026 VDA revisions

From 1 October 2026 the central minimum wage for unskilled construction, sweeping and cleaning, and loading work is ₹840 a day in Area A cities, ₹704 in Area B and ₹565 in Area C. The rates include a variable dearness allowance revised every 1 April and 1 October in line with the consumer price index for industrial workers.

The October order (File No. 6/1/2026-VDA, dated 30 September 2026) followed a rise in the average index from 424.80 to 431.42. The April 2026 revision had already lifted Area A unskilled pay to ₹827 a day. Area A covers the major metros, including Delhi, Greater Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata, Pune, Ahmedabad, Gurgaon and Noida.

Who these rates apply to. Central rates bind employers where the central government is the “appropriate government”: its own undertakings, railways, mines, oil fields, ports, airports and contractors working for them. A private company in most other industries follows its state’s rates. Monthly figures are the daily rate times 26, the method the 2026 central rules prescribe.

07 · The 50% rule

The 50% wages rule and its effect on PF and gratuity

Under the codes, “wages” means basic pay, dearness allowance and retaining allowance. Listed exclusions, such as house rent allowance, conveyance, statutory bonus, overtime and commission, are left out, but if together they exceed 50% of total pay, the excess is added back to wages.

Wages so defined are the base for PF, gratuity, leave encashment and overtime. Indian salaries have long been built with a low basic and large allowances to keep contributions down, so the rule raises the contribution base for many existing structures. A March 2026 government clarification confirmed that overtime counts within the 50% calculation.

Gratuity is the bigger effect, because it is uncapped up to ₹20 lakh and runs off the full wage base. PF is limited by the wage ceiling: contributions are mandatory on wages up to ₹25,000 a month, so in this example the compulsory employer PF rises from ₹2,400 to ₹3,000 a month. Employers can pay on higher wages voluntarily.

08 · Employer cost

What an employer pays on top of the minimum wage

At the Delhi unskilled minimum of ₹18,456 a month, statutory contributions and accruals add about ₹5,423, taking the monthly cost to ₹23,879, or about US$247. That is 29.4% on top of the wage. The figure assumes the whole minimum is paid as basic wages and the establishment has 20 or more employees, so PF and statutory bonus apply.

The mandatory contributions

12% + 1%Employees’ Provident Fund: 12% employer share, part of it to the pension scheme, plus 0.5% EDLI insurance and 0.5% administration. Mandatory on wages up to ₹25,000 a month since 17 September 2026.
3.25%Employees’ State Insurance: employer share for staff earning up to ₹21,000 a month. The employee pays 0.75%. Above the threshold ESI stops.
4.81%Gratuity accrual: 15 days’ wages per year of service (15/26 of a month), payable after five years, or one year for fixed-term staff. Capped at ₹20 lakh.
8.33% to 20%Statutory bonus for employees earning up to ₹21,000 a month in establishments with 20 or more staff, paid within eight months of the year end.

The employee also contributes 12% to PF (₹2,215) and 0.75% to ESI (₹138), so take-home pay at this minimum is about ₹16,103 before any professional tax. Delhi levies none.

The percentage falls as pay rises. ESI stops above ₹21,000 a month, bonus eligibility ends at the same level, and mandatory PF stops growing at ₹25,000. For a salaried professional the statutory load is usually 13% to 18% of basic wages, mostly PF and gratuity. 09 · Bonus

Statutory bonus under the Code on Wages

Every employee earning up to ₹21,000 a month in an establishment with 20 or more staff is entitled to an annual bonus of at least 8.33% of wages, rising to a maximum of 20% depending on the company’s allocable surplus. The bonus provisions of the Code on Wages replaced the Payment of Bonus Act 1965.

For employees earning more than ₹7,000 a month, bonus is calculated on ₹7,000 or the applicable minimum wage, whichever is higher. Notifications of 25 August 2026, applied from 21 November 2025, confirmed both the ₹21,000 eligibility limit and this calculation ceiling. With every main state’s minimum now well above ₹7,000, the minimum wage is in practice the base for most eligible staff.

Not a 13th-month salary. India has no mandatory 13th month. The statutory bonus applies only below the ₹21,000 threshold, must be paid within eight months of the close of the accounting year, and many employers pay it with the festival season. Above the threshold, performance bonuses and variable pay are contractual.

10 · Taxes

Income tax and professional tax at minimum wage level

A worker on any state minimum wage pays no income tax. Under the new tax regime, which is the default, the rebate wipes out tax on taxable income up to ₹12 lakh a year, and the ₹75,000 standard deduction lifts the zero-tax point for salaried staff to ₹12.75 lakh. The 2026 Budget kept the slabs and rebate unchanged.

Employers must still register for tax deduction at source, deduct tax monthly from salaries above the threshold and file quarterly returns. The Income-tax Act 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026, but the salary withholding mechanics are the same.

Professional tax

Many states, including Karnataka, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal and Gujarat, levy a professional tax that the employer deducts from salary and pays to the state. The Constitution caps it at ₹2,500 a year per person, and the slabs and registration rules vary by state. Delhi does not levy it.

11 · Hours and overtime

Working hours and overtime

The labour codes cap work at 8 hours a day and 48 hours a week, with at most six working days in a row before a rest day. Overtime is paid at twice the ordinary rate of wages, needs the worker’s consent and, under the central safety rules, is capped at 144 hours a quarter.

8 / 48Normal hours: 8 a day and 48 a week, with spread-over and daily limits set by state rules
2xOvertime rate: twice the ordinary wage rate, calculated on the code definition of wages
144 hoursOvertime cap per quarter under the central rules, with the worker’s consent

A four-day week is allowed, not required, as long as the weekly cap and overtime rules are met. Office staff in shops and commercial establishments are also covered by their state’s shops and establishments law, which sets opening hours, weekly holidays and sometimes lower overtime caps. Check both.

12 · Leave

Leave entitlements

Annual leave under the safety code is one day for every 20 days worked, for workers with at least 180 working days in the calendar year, or about 15 days a year. Up to 30 days can be carried forward, and leave above that can be encashed. Office staff in most states also get the earned, casual and sick leave set by the state’s shops and establishments law, and employers apply whichever is more generous.

13 · Termination

Notice, retrenchment and final pay

Retrenching a worker under the Industrial Relations Code takes one month’s written notice or pay in lieu, plus compensation of 15 days’ average pay for each completed year of service. The employer must also pay 15 days’ wages per retrenched worker into a new Worker Re-skilling Fund.

14 · Market pay

Minimum wage vs market pay

The minimum wage sets the floor for entry-level, support and blue-collar roles; it is not a benchmark for professional hiring. It matters most in construction, logistics, warehousing, retail, security and facilities work, where many employees are paid at or just above it.

Graduate pay can sit closer to the floor than foreign employers expect. Entry salaries at India’s largest IT services firms are still reported at about ₹3 lakh to ₹3.1 lakh a year (People Matters, February 2026), or roughly ₹25,000 a month. In Bengaluru, Karnataka’s skilled minimum is now ₹29,316 a month, so check how a role is classified before copying an old offer letter.

Cost of living

Living costs differ widely between metros and smaller cities, and housing drives most of the difference. Employers hiring in Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi NCR or Hyderabad rarely pay at the minimum, even for entry roles, because candidates expect more and attrition is costly. In tier-2 cities, pay near the minimum is more common for support roles.

15 · Penalties

Penalties and common compliance mistakes

Paying less than the minimum wage is punishable by a fine of up to ₹50,000 for a first offence under Section 54 of the Code on Wages. A repeat offence within five years can mean imprisonment for up to three months, a fine of up to ₹1 lakh, or both.

Inspectors now act as “inspector-cum-facilitators” and can give employers a chance to fix a breach before prosecution, and first offences punishable only by a fine can be compounded for half the maximum. The employer must still pay arrears, and late PF and ESI contributions carry interest and damages.

Mistakes we see most often

Applying one state’s rates to staff in another. Classifying skill by job title instead of the state’s definitions. Missing a VDA revision, which leaves pay short from the effective date. Keeping basic pay low and topping up with allowances, which can breach both the minimum wage and the 50% rule.

16 · Hiring from abroad

Hiring in India as an international company

A foreign company cannot put employees on its own payroll in India without a registered entity. The options are to set up a private limited company or branch, or to hire through an employer of record that employs staff on your behalf.

Setting up a company takes months and brings PF, ESI, professional tax, shops and establishments registration and labour code filings in every state where you have staff. Our comparison of an EOR vs a private limited company in India sets out the costs and timelines for each route.

Using an employer of record

An EOR is the legal employer in India. It issues appointment letters under the labour codes, runs payroll at the correct state rate and VDA, registers and pays PF, ESI, gratuity and bonus, and handles tax deduction and final settlements. It is often the quickest way to hire remotely, and it shows how an EOR simplifies multi-state compliance.

17 · FAQ

Questions about minimum wage in India

Short answers to what employers ask most about India’s minimum wages, the labour codes and the real cost of a minimum-wage hire, checked against central and state notifications on 24 October 2026.

What is the minimum wage in India in 2026?

There is no single rate. Each state sets its own by skill and zone. For unskilled work in the main hiring states, monthly minimums in October 2026 run from ₹10,558 (West Bengal Zone A) to ₹24,407 (Bengaluru). The central sphere rate for unskilled work in Area A cities is ₹840 a day from 1 October 2026.

Does India have a national minimum wage?

Not yet. The Code on Wages lets the central government set a statutory national floor wage that no state may go below, but it has not been notified. The non-statutory benchmark is still ₹176 a day, set in 2017.

Which state has the highest minimum wage?

Among the main hiring states, Karnataka. Since 22 May 2026 the Bengaluru (Zone 1) minimum is ₹24,407 a month for unskilled work and ₹32,145 for highly skilled work, including VDA.

How often do minimum wages change in India?

Usually twice a year. The central sphere and most states revise the variable dearness allowance on 1 April and 1 October; Maharashtra and West Bengal revise in January and July. Basic rates must be reviewed at least every five years.

What did the labour codes change for minimum wage?

From 21 November 2025 the minimum wage covers every employee, not only listed industries. The codes also introduced a statutory floor wage, a single definition of wages with a 50% cap on excluded allowances, payment deadlines and mandatory appointment letters.

How much does an employer pay above the minimum wage?

At the Delhi unskilled minimum of ₹18,456 a month, PF, EDLI and admin charges, ESI, gratuity accrual and statutory bonus add about ₹5,423, a total of ₹23,879 or 29.4% on top. The share falls at higher salaries as ESI and bonus stop above ₹21,000.

What are the penalties for paying below the minimum wage?

A fine of up to ₹50,000 for a first offence under the Code on Wages. A repeat offence within five years can bring up to three months in jail, a fine of up to ₹1 lakh, or both, plus payment of arrears.

Can you hire in India without a local entity?

Yes. An employer of record can employ staff in India for you, paying the correct state minimum wage and handling PF, ESI, gratuity, bonus, tax and labour code compliance, so you do not need to register a company.
